Feature Trio Grid

Three Claims, Equally Weighted, Never a Fourth

The concept: a centered hero states one argument, and exactly three panels beneath it back it up with three genuinely parallel, equally important reasons — never a card grid sized "as many as we have."

Get Started

Why exactly three

Three is the largest count a reader can hold as "equally weighted" at a glance. A fourth item quietly demotes the other three from a trio to a list — reach for card-catalogue-index once that happens.

Why no card boxes

A bordered, shadowed box implies a boundary content needs. These three claims don't need boundaries from each other — whitespace and a hairline top-rule communicate "distinct but equal" without implying separation that isn't there.

When not to use this

If the three things aren't actually parallel — one matters more, or there are really five — this archetype will flatten a real hierarchy into a fake one. Use magazine-well or bento-mosaic instead, where size is allowed to vary.

The tell that separates a real trio from a padded one: could you swap the order of the three panels and lose nothing? If one clearly has to come first, they were never equal — this archetype only fits content that's genuinely interchangeable in order.
