What Does This Company Actually Do?

The Simple Story

Before a chip goes into a car, a data center, or your phone, it needs to survive years of heat and electrical stress without failing. Aehr builds machines that simulate that stress all at once, testing an entire wafer of chips simultaneously instead of one at a time — like a stress test for a bridge before anyone drives on it, but for silicon.

Who Actually Buys It

Its customers are chipmakers building power semiconductors for electric vehicles, and increasingly, companies building AI processors and data-center optical components. A small number of large customers — as few as five — have historically made up the vast majority of its revenue.

Business Model

How They Make Money

  • Sell wafer-level burn-in and test systems (FOX, WaferPak) to chipmakers
  • Recurring consumable revenue from WaferPak contactors that wear out and need replacing
  • Follow-on orders as customers expand manufacturing capacity on existing platforms

Why the Model Is Clever

  • Wafer-level testing (checking many chips at once) is fundamentally more efficient than testing chips one at a time, giving Aehr a real cost advantage once a customer adopts the approach
  • The same core technology platform serves both the EV/power semiconductor cycle and the AI data-center cycle, diversifying its exposure across two different capex drivers
  • Consumable WaferPak revenue creates a recurring tail alongside the upfront capital equipment sale
Hidden Catalyst Deep Dive
Fiscal 2027 revenue is guided to $130M-$150M — 160% to 200% growth — against a prior Street consensus of roughly $85M, backed by a $100.6M effective backlog already booked, not just forecast.

The Opportunity

Aehr has, in a remarkably short window, transitioned from a company seen as dependent on the silicon carbide EV cycle into one riding two capex buildouts at once: EV power semiconductors and AI data-center infrastructure. Record Q4 fiscal-2026 bookings of $60.7M and an effective backlog of $100.6M give the FY2027 guidance real substance rather than aspiration — and three customers targeting AI and data-center optical markets each already exceeded 10% of Q4 revenue on their own.

Why This Company Wins

Aehr is described as the leader in wafer-level burn-in for silicon carbide, gallium nitride, optical photonics, and memory — a multi-market position that lets it serve both the EV cycle and the AI cycle with variations of the same core platform. That breadth is precisely what let the company pivot so quickly when AI/data-center demand accelerated, without needing to build an entirely new product line from scratch.

Bottom Line Verdict [EDITORIAL OPINION]
The Verdict

Aehr looks like a small, specialized lab that just got called up to run stress tests for two of the biggest infrastructure buildouts of the decade at once — the EV power grid and the AI data center — and the record bookings suggest the call is real, not hopeful thinking.

The long-term case is believable: a $100.6M effective backlog, record Q4 bookings, and a return to quarterly GAAP profitability all point to a genuine inflection, not just a guidance headline. If FY2027 tracks anywhere near the guided $130M-$150M, this remains a company still early in a very large opportunity.

The near-term pain is real: this is still a company with a full-year loss, extreme customer concentration, and a stock that has already rallied 320% pricing in a great deal of that future growth. Investors comfortable with concentration risk and high volatility can reasonably hold through the FY2027 ramp on a 12-24 month horizon; more conservative investors may prefer to wait for the first FY2027 quarterly report to confirm the guidance before adding.
