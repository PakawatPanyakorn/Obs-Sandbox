What Does This Company Actually Do?

The Simple Story

A security camera or a self-driving car's sensor captures huge amounts of video every second — sending all of that to the cloud to be analyzed would be too slow and too expensive. Ambarella makes small, power-sipping AI chips that let the camera or the car understand what it's seeing right there on the spot, instantly, without needing an internet connection.

Who Actually Buys It

Its customers are camera and car makers, security system companies, and increasingly robotics and industrial automation firms building machines that need to "see" and react in real time. A long-term partner, Hanwha Group, now spans security, robotics, and industrial automation applications.

Business Model

How They Make Money

  • Sell Edge AI system-on-chips (SoCs) to camera, automotive, and industrial device makers
  • Higher-margin 5-nanometer AI SoCs are driving average selling prices (ASP) higher across both new and existing markets
  • New robotics and HAI (physical/robotic AI) infrastructure design wins expanding beyond the traditional camera-chip base

Why the Model Is Clever

  • Both unit volume and ASP are each contributing roughly half of overall revenue growth — a healthier mix than volume growth alone
  • With 42M+ Edge AI SoCs already shipped and ~$1B in cumulative Edge AI revenue, Ambarella has a real track record that's hard for a new entrant to replicate quickly
  • A single chip platform architecture serves automotive, security, and robotics markets, spreading R&D costs across multiple growth vectors
Hidden Catalyst Deep Dive
A long-term Hanwha Group agreement covering security, robotics, and industrial automation carries a potential revenue opportunity of up to $800 million over more than a decade — though that figure is not guaranteed.

The Opportunity

Ambarella has quietly shifted from being seen as a camera-chip supplier into a broader "physical AI" platform company. Robotics design wins have already topped 15, with lifetime revenue above $100M and more than 30 customers still in the pipeline — and the company just achieved its first HAI infrastructure design win with the N1655 SoC, targeting AI appliances that use large-language-model-powered search and multimodal intelligence.

Why This Company Wins

Rosenblatt Securities named Ambarella one of its top technology picks for the second half of 2026, specifically citing its positioning in the fast-growing physical AI market, and maintained a $120 price target. With 370+ customer AI projects already in production, Ambarella brings a depth of real-world deployment experience that a robotics-focused newcomer without an existing camera-chip customer base would need years to build.

Bottom Line Verdict [EDITORIAL OPINION]
The Verdict

Ambarella looks like a specialist instrument-maker who spent a decade perfecting one tool — on-device vision AI — and is now watching that exact tool get pulled into car dashboards, security systems, and robots all at once.

The long-term case is believable: a real track record of 42M+ chips shipped and 370+ production deployments, record automotive sales, and a Hanwha partnership that — even discounted heavily for its "not guaranteed" caveat — represents a genuinely large new market. If robotics design wins keep converting to revenue the way the first 15+ have, the growth story extends well past automotive and security cameras.

The near-term pain is real: this is still an unprofitable company at a premium valuation, concentrated through a single fulfillment partner, and FY2027 growth guidance of 10-15% is a real deceleration from FY2026's 37%. Investors comfortable with valuation and concentration risk can reasonably hold through the robotics/Hanwha ramp on a 12-24 month horizon; more conservative investors may prefer to wait for GAAP profitability or a valuation reset before adding.
