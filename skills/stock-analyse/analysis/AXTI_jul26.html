What Does This Company Actually Do?

The Simple Story

Some materials conduct electricity and light better than ordinary silicon. AXT grows near-perfect crystals of these special materials — indium phosphide and gallium arsenide — then slices them into thin wafers. Those wafers become the base layer for lasers, chips, and sensors that move data inside AI data centers, connect satellites, and power 5G networks.

Who Actually Buys It

Its customers are chipmakers and laser/optics manufacturers who build the actual finished components — think companies making the optical transceivers that link servers inside AI data centers, satellite makers, and 5G equipment suppliers. A large and growing share of demand now comes from within China itself.

Business Model

How They Make Money

  • Grow and sell InP and GaAs substrate wafers to chip and laser manufacturers
  • Germanium substrates sold into the satellite/space solar-cell market
  • Raw material and byproduct sales through its vertically integrated Chinese subsidiary, Tongmei

Why the Model Is Clever

  • Vertically integrated: AXT designs its own crystal-growth furnaces and controls key raw materials rather than buying them from third parties
  • Repurposing existing GaAs facilities to accelerate InP production means faster capacity growth without building entirely new plants
  • Domestic China shipments require no export permit — a structural way to keep growing even if international permits slow down
Hidden Catalyst Deep Dive

The Opportunity

AI data centers move enormous amounts of data between servers using optical transceivers, and those transceivers need indium phosphide lasers. AXT's InP revenue surged to $13.6M in Q1 2026, its record backlog now exceeds $100M, and the company is racing to double InP capacity in 2026 versus Q4 2025, with a second doubling planned for 2027. Crucially, a large and growing share of this demand is domestic Chinese demand — China's InP laser market revenue more than doubled sequentially in Q1 2026 and may double again in Q2 — and domestic shipments require no U.S. or Chinese export permit at all.

Why This Company Wins

AXT's local manufacturing presence through Tongmei means it can serve China's own push to build a domestic AI and semiconductor supply chain directly, sidestepping the export-permit bottleneck that governs its international shipments. Combined with a vertically integrated crystal-growth process and a freshly closed $550M capital raise funding capacity expansion, AXT is positioned to capture demand on both sides of the export-control line.

Bottom Line Verdict [EDITORIAL OPINION]
The Verdict

AXT looks like a quiet materials supplier that suddenly found itself standing at the exact intersection of two of 2026's biggest stories — AI data-center buildout and U.S.-China tech competition — and is now trying to prove it can serve both without getting caught in the crossfire.

The long-term case is believable: a record InP backlog, a fully funded and ahead-of-schedule capacity expansion, and a genuinely clever hedge in China's domestic demand that doesn't need an export permit at all. If Q2 2026 confirms the guided record InP quarter, the growth story becomes much harder to dismiss.

The near-term pain is real: this is still an unprofitable company with a stock that has already made an extraordinary move and just took its first analyst target cut. Investors comfortable with extreme volatility and single-country geopolitical risk can reasonably hold through the InP capacity ramp on a 12-24 month horizon; more conservative investors may want to wait for tomorrow's Q2 print and a couple of quarters of consistent execution before adding.
