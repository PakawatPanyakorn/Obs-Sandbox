What Does This Company Actually Do?

The Simple Story

When a chipmaker stacks multiple memory chips on top of each other to build something like HBM (the ultra-fast memory that feeds AI chips), a single microscopic flaw can ruin the whole stack. Camtek builds the high-resolution camera-and-measurement systems that inspect every layer as it's built, catching defects before a chip becomes an expensive paperweight.

Who Actually Buys It

Its customers are chipmakers and outsourced assembly and test companies (OSATs) — the factories that package raw silicon into finished chips. Increasingly, its biggest customers are memory makers building HBM for AI accelerators and the OSATs assembling advanced AI chip packages for companies like Nvidia's supply chain.

Business Model

How They Make Money

  • Sell inspection and 3D metrology systems (Eagle, Hawk platforms) to chipmakers and OSATs
  • Multi-system orders tied to new fab or packaging line buildouts, often booked a year or more ahead of delivery
  • Recurring parts, service, and upgrade revenue on its installed equipment base

Why the Model Is Clever

  • Combines 2D inspection and 3D metrology on a single platform — fewer tools needed on a customer's line, and stickier once qualified into a process
  • Every new HBM or advanced packaging line needs inspection tooling, so unit growth in AI packaging translates fairly directly into equipment orders
  • A strong $850M balance sheet gives it room to keep investing in R&D through cyclical downturns rather than cutting engineering when times get tough
Hidden Catalyst Deep Dive

The Opportunity

As AI accelerators demand more memory bandwidth, chipmakers are racing to scale production of HBM (high-bandwidth memory), which is built by stacking multiple memory dies with precision interconnects. Camtek's Hawk platform, paired with its Eagle G5 system, is purpose-built to inspect and measure these complex multi-die stacks. In June 2026 alone, Camtek secured $105M in new multi-system orders from a tier-1 OSAT and a leading HBM manufacturer, and its CEO has confirmed $260M in total HBM-related orders spanning 2026 and 2027.

Why This Company Wins

Camtek's combined 2D inspection + 3D metrology capability on one platform is purpose-built for the complex, multi-layer stacking process HBM production requires — an area where older-generation inspection tools, including some from larger rival KLA-Tencor, are less optimized for the newest packaging architectures. AI-related products already make up roughly half of Camtek's revenue, and Hawk/Eagle G5 revenue is expected to double in 2026 alone.

Bottom Line Verdict [EDITORIAL OPINION]
The Verdict

Camtek looks like a specialist inspector who just got hired onto the biggest construction project in tech — the AI data-center memory buildout — and is now proving it can handle the volume without cutting corners.

The long-term case is believable: a technically differentiated inspection platform purpose-built for HBM's hardest packaging steps, a confirmed $260M order book running through 2027, and management's own guidance for a 25%+ revenue acceleration in H2 2026. If margin recovers to the promised ~30% alongside that growth, the current valuation could look reasonable in hindsight.

The near-term pain is real: margin just compressed sharply, China exposure sits directly in the path of export-control policy, and a 133x trailing P/E leaves little room for the H2 story to disappoint. Investors comfortable with China-exposure and semiconductor-cycle risk can reasonably hold through the H2 2026 margin recovery on a 12-18 month horizon; more conservative investors may prefer to wait for the August 10 print to confirm the margin trajectory before adding.
