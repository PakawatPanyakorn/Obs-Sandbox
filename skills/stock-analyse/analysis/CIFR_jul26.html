What Does This Company Actually Do?

The Simple Story

Cipher Digital used to run huge warehouses full of computers that solved puzzles to earn Bitcoin. Now it's turning those same buildings — and the expensive power hookups it already built — into data centers that AI companies rent by the year, the same way a landlord rents out floor space instead of running a factory on it.

Who Actually Buys It

The customers are hyperscale cloud and AI infrastructure companies: Amazon Web Services and Fluidstack, an AI cloud provider backed by Google. They sign 10-15 year leases for guaranteed blocks of power and floor space to run their own AI computers, instead of spending years building new sites from scratch.

Business Model

How They Make Money

  • Legacy Bitcoin mining at its Odessa, Texas site — a shrinking share of revenue as capacity gets redirected
  • Long-term hyperscale data-center leases — fixed rent plus power pass-through from AWS and Fluidstack over 10-15 year terms

Why the Model Is Clever

  • Converts a boom-bust commodity business — bitcoin mining margins swing with the price of Bitcoin — into contracted, multi-year rent income
  • Reuses power infrastructure and land already built for mining, cutting years off the timeline a hyperscaler would normally need to build a data center from scratch
Hidden Catalyst Deep Dive

The Opportunity

Cipher is converting its legacy bitcoin-mining power sites — already wired into the Texas grid — into AI/HPC data centers leased to hyperscalers. That single change moves the same megawatts from crypto-mining economics to data-center economics: three leases across 907MW already lock in roughly $11.4B in contracted base-term revenue, and management guides toward ~$870M in annual run-rate revenue with ~76% blended EBITDA margins once fully energized.

Why This Company Wins

Cipher already owns the land, power interconnects, and grid capacity that a hyperscaler would otherwise spend years permitting and building from scratch — repurposing it is faster and cheaper than greenfield construction. Google holds an equity stake in Cipher and has agreed to backstop the Fluidstack lease if Fluidstack can't perform, which meaningfully reduces counterparty risk most data-center landlords don't have.

Every megawatt Cipher wired for mining Bitcoin is now wired for renting to Amazon and Google's AI cloud partner instead — the infrastructure didn't change, only who's paying rent on it.

Bottom Line Verdict [EDITORIAL OPINION]
The Verdict

Cipher Digital is like a bitcoin miner who spent years building a warehouse full of expensive power hookups, and just realized landlords make steadier money renting that space out than gamblers do mining crypto with it.

The green case is real: $11.4B in contracted hyperscale revenue, an AWS lease going live in August 2026, and a Google-backed second tenant starting in October give Cipher a growth runway most bitcoin miners never had, at guided margins (~76% EBITDA) far above mining economics.

The near-term pain is also real: nearly $4.7B in debt, a company still losing almost $900M a year, heavy insider selling, and a stock price that now swings with AI-sector sentiment as much as company news. Long-term growth investors comfortable with volatility should think in a 12-24 month horizon tied to the lease-energization dates; short-term traders should treat the Aug 4 earnings print and the Aug/Oct 2026 energization windows as the key dates that will move the stock either way.
