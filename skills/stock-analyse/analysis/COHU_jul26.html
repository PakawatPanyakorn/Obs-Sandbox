What Does This Company Actually Do?

The Simple Story

Before a chip leaves the factory, it has to pass a final exam — a battery of electrical tests to make sure it works. Cohu makes the robotic arms and conveyor-like systems that pick up each chip, position it precisely, and feed it into the test machine at high speed, plus the inspection cameras that check for physical defects along the way.

Who Actually Buys It

Its customers are semiconductor manufacturers and outsourced test subcontractors (OSATs) who need to test huge volumes of chips efficiently. Increasingly, its customers include companies testing AI data-center processors and high-bandwidth memory, a newer and faster-growing category alongside its traditional automotive and industrial chip customers.

Business Model

How They Make Money

  • Sell test handler, inspection, and thermal subsystem equipment to chipmakers and OSATs
  • Recurring revenue from test contactors, consumables, and service on its large installed base — roughly 60% of sales
  • New semiconductor test systems and HBM inspection tools sold into the growing AI/HPC category

Why the Model Is Clever

  • A ~60% recurring revenue base smooths out the normal boom-bust cycle of semiconductor capital equipment spending
  • Active thermal control on its Eclipse handler platform is specifically suited to the higher power densities of AI datacenter chips, extending a proven platform into a new growth category
  • Top-three position with an estimated 20-28% share of the $2.1B test handler market gives real scale and customer relationships to build the newer AI/HPC business on top of
Hidden Catalyst Deep Dive

The Opportunity

Cohu has built a genuine HPC opportunity pipeline of roughly $750M — about $650M in test handlers and $100M in HBM inspection — and it's already converting that pipeline into real orders, not just projections. The company recently secured its second multi-unit order specifically for testing next-generation AI datacenter processors, and semiconductor test orders overall were up 163% year-over-year in Q1 2026.

Why This Company Wins

Cohu's Eclipse handler platform, built around active thermal control, was engineered to manage the intense heat that comes with higher power chips — a design advantage that translates directly to AI datacenter processors, which run far hotter than typical logic chips. Combined with an already-installed base and customer relationships from its traditional test handler business, Cohu doesn't need to win AI/HPC customers from a standing start.

Bottom Line Verdict [EDITORIAL OPINION]
The Verdict

Cohu looks like a veteran contractor who just landed the biggest renovation job of its career — the tools and crew are proven, the order book just got a lot bigger, and now it's a question of whether the invoices catch up to the work.

The long-term case is believable: a top-three market position, a $750M HPC pipeline larger than current annual revenue, and real order wins — not just projections — converting into multi-unit deals for AI datacenter processor testing. If guidance beats continue through the rest of 2026, the growth story looks solid on its own merits.

The near-term pain is real: profitability has lagged revenue growth for several quarters now, and the company hasn't given a fixed date for a return to consistent GAAP profit. Investors comfortable with execution risk on the path to profitability can reasonably hold through the HPC ramp on a 12-18 month horizon; more conservative investors may prefer to wait for a clean profitable quarter before adding.
